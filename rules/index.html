Sequence game rules: how to play, explained simply

In Sequence, you play a card from your hand and place your chip on a board space showing that card. The first player or team to make the lines of five you need wins: two lines when there are 2 sides, one line when there are 3. Two-eyed jacks are wild, and one-eyed jacks remove an opponent's chip.

These rules work for the boxed board game and for Chipline, our free online version you can play in your browser with friends or against the computer.

How to play Sequence in 6 steps

Each turn you play one card, place one chip, and draw one card. Keep going around the table until a side completes the lines it needs.

  1. Form sides and deal. Split into 2 or 3 sides (players or teams). Sit so the teams alternate around the table. Deal each player the number of cards shown in the player table, from 7 cards each with 2 players down to 3 each with 10 or 12.
  2. Play a card. On your turn, play one card face up and put one of your chips on a free board space that shows the same card. Each card is on the board twice, so pick the space that helps you most.
  3. Draw a card. Take a new card from the deck to end your turn, so your hand stays the same size. (In Chipline this happens automatically.)
  4. Use jacks. Jacks are not on the board. A two-eyed jack lets you place a chip anywhere free; a one-eyed jack removes an opponent's chip. See jacks.
  5. Swap dead cards. If both spaces for a card are already taken, swap it for a new card at the start of your turn. See dead cards.
  6. Make your lines. Five of your chips in a straight line is a sequence. The corners count for everyone. The first side to make the sequences it needs wins.

One-eyed and two-eyed jacks

Two-eyed jacks (jack of diamonds and jack of clubs) are wild: place your chip on any free space. One-eyed jacks (jack of spades and jack of hearts) remove one opponent chip from the board, as long as it is not part of a finished sequence.

Using a one-eyed jack takes your whole turn: you remove the chip but do not place one of your own. Save wild jacks to finish a line or to block an opponent who has four in a row.

The four corners

The four corner spaces are free and count as a chip of every colour. Any side can use a corner as one of the five spaces in its line, and no one ever places a chip there.

How to win: sequences of five

A sequence is five of your chips in a straight line: across, down or diagonally. With 2 sides you need 2 sequences; with 3 sides you need 1. Your second sequence may reuse one chip from your first.

Once a sequence is complete, its chips are safe: one-eyed jacks cannot remove them.

Dead cards

A dead card is a card whose two board spaces are both already covered, so it can never be played. At the start of your turn, put it on the discard pile, draw a replacement, and then play as normal. You can swap one dead card per turn.

Other numbers (5, 7 and 11) do not split into equal teams, so the usual fix is for one player to sit out each game or to swap in and out between games.

Sequence rules for 2 players

Each player gets 7 cards and needs 2 sequences. It is the most strategic version: with only one opponent, every block matters, so watch for four in a row and keep a jack ready.

Sequence rules for 3 players

Each player gets 6 cards and plays alone with their own colour. Only one sequence wins, so games are quicker and you must block whichever player is closest to five.

Sequence rules for 4 players

Play 2 teams of 2, sitting so partners are not next to each other. Each player gets 6 cards and a team needs 2 sequences. Partners share a colour but cannot talk about their cards.

Team games for 6 to 12 players

Big groups split into 2 or 3 equal teams, seated so the teams alternate. Hands get smaller as the group grows (5 cards with 6 players, 3 cards with 10 or 12), so plan with your teammates' chips, not just your own. In Chipline every player can join from their own phone with one link.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting to draw. Always end your turn by drawing, or you play the rest of the game with fewer cards.
  • Using a one-eyed jack on a finished line. Chips in a completed sequence cannot be removed.
  • Wasting a wild jack early. A two-eyed jack is strongest when it completes a line or blocks one.
  • Table talk. Teammates may not say what cards they hold or hint at a space.
  • Ignoring the corners. A line that runs into a corner needs only four of your chips.

Questions about the rules of Sequence

How many cards are in a Sequence game?

Sequence uses two standard 52-card decks, so 104 cards in total, including 8 jacks. The board has 100 spaces: two of every card except the jacks (96 spaces) plus 4 free corners.

How many players can play Sequence?

From 2 to 12 players. Two or three people play on their own. With more players you play in 2 or 3 teams, so the player count must split evenly: 4, 6, 8, 9, 10 or 12.

How many sequences do you need to win?

With 2 players or 2 teams you need two sequences. With 3 players or 3 teams you need only one.

Do the corners count in Sequence?

Yes. The four corner spaces count as a chip of every colour, so any side can use a corner as one of the five spaces in a line.

Can two sequences share a chip?

Yes, one chip. Your second sequence may reuse one chip from your first sequence, but not more than one.

What is a dead card?

A card whose two board spaces are both already covered. Discard it at the start of your turn, draw a replacement, then play as normal.

Can teammates talk about their cards?

No. Teammates may not tell each other what cards they hold or hint at which space to play.
