Sequence game corner rules: how the four free corners work

Published 25 November 2026 · Chipline

In Sequence, the four corner spaces are free. Nobody plays a card or places a chip there, and each corner counts as a chip of every color at the same time. Any player or team can use a corner as one of the five spaces in a sequence, so a line that runs into a corner needs only four chips.

Key takeaways

  • The board has 4 free corners and 96 card spaces. No card matches a corner, and no chip is ever placed there.
  • A corner counts for everyone at once. You and your opponents can all build lines through the same corner.
  • A sequence through a corner needs only 4 of your chips. There are 12 such lines on the board, three per corner.
  • A corner cannot be removed by a one-eyed jack or taken by a two-eyed jack.
  • A corner can be the one shared space between your two sequences, just like any other space.

What are the corners in the Sequence game?

The Sequence board is a 10x10 grid with 100 spaces. Ninety-six of them show a playing card: every card in the deck except the jacks, printed twice. The remaining four spaces are the corners, and they show no card at all. They are usually marked with a symbol, and in Chipline they carry a star.

Because no card matches a corner, you can never play a card to put a chip there. That is the whole point: the corners are already "filled" for everyone from the first turn. The board layout guide explains how the other 96 spaces are arranged.

Do the corners count for everyone in Sequence?

Yes. A corner acts as a chip of every color at the same time. It does not belong to whoever reaches it first, and using it does not stop anyone else from using it too.

So if your team builds a line along the top row that ends in the top-left corner, an opponent can still build a line down the left column that also ends in that same corner. Both lines are legal, and both use the corner as one of their five spaces.

This works the same way in every format: 2 players, 3 players, or 2 or 3 teams in a bigger group.

How many chips do you need for a sequence through a corner?

Only four. A sequence is five spaces in a straight line across, down or diagonally, and the corner supplies one of them for free.

Each corner sits at the end of exactly three possible five-space lines: one along the edge row, one along the edge column, and one along the long diagonal toward the center. With four corners, that makes 12 lines on the board that need only four chips.

Do you need a card to use a corner?

No. You never play a card for a corner and you never put a chip on one. The corner simply counts as part of your line once your four chips are in place. Play the cards that match the other four spaces as normal.

The jacks follow the same logic:

  • Two-eyed jacks (diamonds and clubs) let you place a chip on any open card space. A corner is not an open card space, so you cannot put a wild chip there, and you do not need to.
  • One-eyed jacks (spades and hearts) remove one opponent chip. There is no chip on a corner, so a corner can never be removed or blocked this way.

More detail on both jacks is in one-eyed vs two-eyed jacks.

Can you use the same corner in two sequences?

Yes. When you need two sequences to win (2 players or 2 teams), your second sequence may share one space with your first. A corner counts like any other space for this rule, so it can be that one shared space.

For example, you could complete the top row from the corner to the fifth space, then complete the left column from the same corner down to the fifth space. That is two sequences for only eight chips. What you cannot do is share the corner and another space: the overlap is limited to one space in total.

With 3 players or 3 teams, only one sequence is needed to win, so this rule rarely comes up. See our guide to Sequence with 3 players for that format.

How do you use the corners to win more often?

Corner lines are cheap, but they are also easy to spot, and your opponents get the same discount. A few habits help:

  1. Check all three lines at every corner. Players notice the edge rows quickly but often miss the long diagonal toward the center.
  2. Count to four, not five. When an opponent has three chips on a corner line, they are one card away from a sequence. Treat it like four in a row anywhere else.
  3. Block early on corner lines. One chip of yours anywhere on those four spaces kills that line for the other side. Blocking costs the same as anywhere else but stops a cheaper threat.
  4. Plan a corner double. In a two-sequence game, two lines that meet at one corner need eight chips. Building both at once also creates two threats an opponent cannot block with one move.
  5. Do not ignore the center. Corner lines are fast, but edge spaces sit on fewer possible lines than central spaces. Mix corner threats with lines through the middle.

What are common mistakes with the corner rule?

  • Putting a chip on a corner. Corners never take chips. If a chip is sitting there, take it back and replay the card on a matching space.
  • Thinking a corner belongs to one side. It counts for every player and team, even after someone has used it.
  • Counting the corner as a sixth space. A line from the corner plus four chips is already five. You do not need five chips beside it.
  • Trying to remove a corner with a one-eyed jack. There is nothing to remove, so the corner is always safe.
  • Forgetting corners when blocking. Three chips next to a corner is as dangerous as four chips in the middle of the board.

Frequently asked questions

Do the corners count in Sequence?

Yes. The four corner spaces are free and count as a chip of every color, so any player or team can use a corner as one of the five spaces in a sequence.

Can both teams use the same corner?

Yes. A corner never belongs to one side. Two or three teams can each build a line through the same corner, even at the same time.

Do you put a chip on the corner in Sequence?

No. No card matches a corner, so nobody ever places a chip there. The corner counts as part of your line once your other four chips are in place.

How many chips do you need for a sequence with a corner?

Four. The corner supplies the fifth space. Each corner starts three such lines: along the edge row, along the edge column and along the diagonal, for 12 in total.

Can a one-eyed jack remove a corner?

No. One-eyed jacks remove an opponent's chip, and there is never a chip on a corner, so corners cannot be removed or blocked.

Can two sequences share a corner?

Yes. Your two sequences may share one space, and a corner can be that space. They cannot share the corner and another space as well.
