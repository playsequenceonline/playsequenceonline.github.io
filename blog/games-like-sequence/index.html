Games like Sequence: 8 picks for your next game night

Published November 20, 2026 · Chipline

The best games like Sequence are line-building games where you race to connect pieces while blocking opponents. Gomoku and Pente share the five-in-a-row goal, Connect Four is the quick family version, and Qwirkle, Blokus and Rummikub mix tactics with tile luck. For the same cards-plus-board feel, try a Sequence-style game online.

Key takeaways

  • Closest in goal: Gomoku and Pente, which are also five-in-a-row games on a grid.
  • Best for young kids: Connect Four and tic-tac-toe teach the same "build and block" idea.
  • Most like Sequence's mix of luck and tactics: Qwirkle and Rummikub, where you plan around the tiles you draw.
  • Few alternatives handle big groups. Sequence plays in teams for up to 12 people; most of these games top out at 4.
  • Want Sequence itself without the box? Play a Sequence-style game free in your browser.

What makes Sequence fun, and what should a similar game have?

Before picking an alternative, it helps to name what you like about Sequence. Most people point to a few things:

  1. A simple goal. Get five of your chips in a row on a 10x10 board. Anyone can understand it in a minute.
  2. Building and blocking at the same time. Every move either extends your line or stops someone else's.
  3. A little luck from the cards. You can only place a chip on a space that matches a card in your hand, so even a beginner can win.
  4. Special cards that swing the game. Two-eyed jacks are wild, and one-eyed jacks remove an opponent's chip.
  5. Team play. Two players or teams need two sequences; three players or teams need one. With partners, you share a color and a plan without talking strategy out loud.

No other game hits all five, but each pick below shares at least two. If you need a refresher first, the Sequence rules page covers setup, jacks and dead cards.

Which games like Sequence have five in a row?

Gomoku

Gomoku is the classic five-in-a-row game. Two players take turns placing black and white stones on the crossing points of a grid, and the first to get exactly five (or at least five, depending on the rule set) in a straight line wins. It is often played on a Go board, but a sheet of graph paper and two pens work just as well.

Compared with Sequence, Gomoku has no cards, so you can play anywhere you like. That makes it pure strategy: the stronger player wins far more often. If you enjoy the "two threats at once" moment in Sequence, Gomoku is that moment on every turn.

Pente

Pente builds on Gomoku with one big twist: you can capture. If you trap exactly two of your opponent's stones between two of yours, you remove them. You win by making five in a row or by capturing five pairs.

Captures feel a lot like Sequence's one-eyed jacks. A line that looks safe can be broken, so you have to protect your stones as well as build. Pente is played on a 19x19 grid and is usually a two-player game, though team variants exist.

What is a good game like Sequence for kids?

Connect Four

Connect Four is many children's first line game. Players drop discs into a standing frame with 7 columns and 6 rows, and the first to connect four in a row, across, up or on a diagonal, wins. Gravity means you can only play on top of a stack, which keeps choices simple.

It teaches the exact skill Sequence rewards: noticing when your opponent has three in a row and blocking before it is too late. Games take a few minutes, so it is easy to play "best of five."

Tic-tac-toe

For the youngest players, tic-tac-toe on a 3x3 grid is the starting point. Once a child stops letting you win with an obvious three in a row, they are ready for Connect Four, and then for a full game of Sequence on a team with an adult.

Sequence itself also works for kids who can match cards. Pair a younger child with an older player on the same team, and let them find the matching spaces while the partner thinks about blocking.

Which games like Sequence mix luck and strategy?

Qwirkle

Qwirkle uses wooden tiles in 6 colors and 6 shapes. On your turn you add tiles to the shared grid to make lines that share a color or a shape, and you score for every line you add to. Like Sequence, you are limited by what you drew, and like Sequence, a good spot for you is often a good spot for an opponent too. It plays well with 2 to 4 people.

Rummikub

Rummikub is a tile game that plays like rummy. You make runs of the same color and groups of the same number, and you can rearrange tiles already on the table. It shares Sequence's feel of "I have the right piece, but where does it fit best?" and works for 2 to 4 players.

Blokus

Blokus has no luck at all, but it scratches the same territorial itch. Each player has 21 pieces of one color and tries to place as many as possible on a 20x20 board. Your pieces may only touch your own color at the corners, so the game becomes a fight for space, and blocking a rival's path is half the fun. Up to 4 people can play.

How is Sequence different from these games?

Two things set Sequence apart. First, the card hand: you cannot just place a chip anywhere, so you have to balance the best space on the board with what you are holding. Second, teams. Most line games are strictly one against one, but Sequence plays naturally in 2 or 3 teams, which is why it works for family gatherings. Our guide to playing Sequence with 6 to 12 players shows how the teams and card counts work.

The jacks matter too. A two-eyed jack lets you play anywhere and a one-eyed jack lets you undo an opponent's progress, so a game is rarely over until the last chip. For when to use each one, read one-eyed vs two-eyed jacks.

How do you pick the right game for your group?

  • Two strategy fans: Gomoku or Pente.
  • A parent and a young child: tic-tac-toe, then Connect Four.
  • Three or four adults who like a bit of luck: Qwirkle or Rummikub.
  • A big family group or a party: Sequence in teams, at the table or online.
  • Friends in different cities: a free browser game you can join from a link.

Whichever you choose, the habits carry over: watch for your opponent's open lines, build two threats at once, and use the edges and corners. The Sequence strategy tips and the FAQ go deeper on all of these.

Frequently asked questions

What game is most like Sequence?

Gomoku and Pente are closest in goal, because you also try to get five in a row on a grid. Neither uses cards, though. If you want the cards, jacks and team play too, a Sequence-style game such as Chipline is the closest match.

Is Sequence like Connect Four?

Partly. Both are about making a line before your opponent and blocking theirs. Connect Four needs four in a row and has no cards, so every move is open; Sequence needs five in a row and your cards limit where you can play.

What is a good game like Sequence for 2 players?

Gomoku, Pente and Connect Four are all built for two players and reward the same habit of building one line while blocking another. Sequence itself also works well head-to-head: each player gets 7 cards and needs two sequences to win.

Are there games like Sequence for big groups?

Few line-building games go past 4 players. Sequence itself is one of the best options, because it is played in 2 or 3 teams and handles up to 12 people. Rummikub and Qwirkle are better kept to 2 to 4 players.
