What is Sequence? The card and board game explained

Published 23 November 2026 · Chipline

Sequence is a strategy game that mixes playing cards with a board. Each card in your hand matches a space on a 10x10 board printed with playing cards. You play a card, place a chip on its space, and try to make rows of five chips (a "sequence") while blocking opponents. It suits 2 to 12 players, in teams.

Key takeaways

  • Sequence is a card-and-board game: the cards in your hand tell you where you are allowed to place chips.
  • A sequence is 5 chips in a row, across, down or diagonally. The 4 corners are free spaces that count for everyone.
  • Two-eyed jacks are wild; one-eyed jacks remove an opponent's chip.
  • Two players or two teams need two sequences to win; three players or three teams need one.
  • It is a mix of luck and skill: the deal is random, but choosing where to build and when to block decides most games.

What kind of game is Sequence?

Sequence sits somewhere between a card game and a classic "get five in a row" board game. If you know Connect Four or tic-tac-toe, the goal will feel familiar: line up your pieces before your opponent does. The twist is that you cannot place a chip anywhere you like. You may only place it on a space that matches a card you play from your hand.

That one rule turns a simple line-building game into a game of planning, hand management and reading the board. You are always asking two questions: "Which of my cards helps my line?" and "Which space must I take before the other side gets it?"

It is usually played as a family or party game, because the rules take only a few minutes to explain and anyone who knows a standard deck of cards can join in. It has been a game-night staple since the 1980s.

How does Sequence work?

On your turn you play one card, put a chip on a matching space, and draw a new card. Players keep taking turns until someone completes the number of sequences they need to win.

  1. Form teams and deal. Players split into two or three teams (or play alone with 2 or 3 players). Each player gets a hand of cards based on the number of players, from 7 cards each with 2 players down to 3 each with 10 or 12.
  2. Play a card. Choose a card from your hand and put it face up on your own discard pile.
  3. Place a chip. Put one of your chips on either of the two board spaces showing that card, as long as it is open.
  4. Draw a card. Take the top card from the draw pile so your hand stays the same size. Then the next player goes.
  5. Make sequences. Five of your chips in a straight line (across, down or diagonal) is a sequence. Once a sequence is complete, those chips are safe.
  6. Win. The first player or team to make the number of sequences needed wins the game.

You can talk and joke at the table, but you are not allowed to tell or hint to teammates what cards you hold. That keeps team games fair and makes reading your partner's moves part of the fun. The full rules, including setup details, are on our Sequence rules page.

What do the jacks do in Sequence?

The jacks are the special cards in Sequence, and there are two kinds:

  • Two-eyed jacks (diamonds and clubs) are wild. Play one to place a chip on any open space on the board.
  • One-eyed jacks (spades and hearts) remove a chip. Play one to take an opponent's chip off the board. You cannot remove a chip that is already part of a completed sequence, and you do not place a chip of your own that turn.

Knowing when to use a jack, and when to hold it, is one of the biggest skill gaps between new and experienced players. Our post on one-eyed vs two-eyed jacks explains how to tell them apart and when to play each.

Is Sequence a game of luck or skill?

Both. The luck comes from the cards: you cannot choose what you are dealt or what you draw, and a well-timed wild jack can swing a game. But the skill shows up on every single turn:

  • Choosing which of two spaces to take. Every card has two spaces, and the better one is often the space that helps you and blocks the other side at the same time.
  • Blocking at the right moment. Spotting an opponent's four-in-a-row (or a line of three with open ends) before it becomes a sequence.
  • Saving jacks. A one-eyed jack held back for an emergency often wins more games than one played early.
  • Team play. Without talking about your cards, you learn to build on the lines your partner starts.

In practice, an experienced player beats a beginner far more often than chance alone would explain, but a beginner can still win on a good night. That balance is a big part of why it works so well for mixed-age family games. Our Sequence strategy tips cover blocking, double threats and jack timing in more detail.

Is Sequence hard to learn?

No. Most people pick it up after one or two rounds. If you can recognize playing cards, you already know most of what you need. The only rules that new players tend to mix up are:

  • Which jacks are wild and which remove chips (two eyes = wild, one eye = remove).
  • That corners count for everyone.
  • What to do with a dead card, a card whose two spaces are both already covered. You can swap it once per turn for a new card. See Sequence dead card rules.

Children who can match card faces can usually play, especially on a team with an adult. The retail box is generally marked for ages 7 and up, and younger kids often enjoy it as a partner.

How many people can play Sequence?

Sequence works for 2 to 12 players. With 2 or 3 people, everyone plays alone. With more, players split into two or three equal teams, and teammates sit so that turns alternate between teams around the table. That is why player counts like 5, 7 and 11 need a small house rule or one player sitting out. Our guide to playing Sequence with a big group covers seating and odd numbers.

How long does a game of Sequence take?

A typical game is short enough to play several in an evening. Two-player games tend to be quicker because there are fewer turns between yours, while large team games take longer as each player waits for their turn and teams block each other more. Playing online with a turn timer is an easy way to keep big games moving.

Frequently asked questions

What is the goal of Sequence?

The goal is to make rows of five of your chips, called sequences, on the board before the other players or teams. Two players or two teams need two sequences to win; three players or three teams need one.

Is Sequence a card game or a board game?

It is both. You play cards from your hand, and each card lets you place a chip on a matching space of a 10x10 board printed with playing cards.

Can you play Sequence with 2 players?

Yes. Each player gets 7 cards and needs two sequences to win. It is one of the most strategic ways to play because you control your whole side.

What do the corners do in Sequence?

The four corner spaces are free. They count as a chip of every player's color, so any player can use a corner as one of the five spaces in a sequence.

What is the difference between one-eyed and two-eyed jacks?

Two-eyed jacks (diamonds and clubs) are wild and let you place a chip on any open space. One-eyed jacks (spades and hearts) let you remove an opponent's chip that is not part of a completed sequence.
