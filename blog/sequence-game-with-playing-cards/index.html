How to play the Sequence game with playing cards

Published 26 November 2026 · Chipline

Yes, you can play Sequence with regular playing cards. You need four standard 52-card decks: two decks with the jacks removed (96 cards) laid face up in a 10x10 grid with four empty corners as the board, and two full decks (104 cards, no jokers) to draw from. Coins or buttons work as chips.

Key takeaways

  • The draw deck in Sequence is just two ordinary decks shuffled together: 104 cards, jokers removed.
  • The only special part is the board. Two more decks without their jacks give exactly 96 cards, one for every non-corner space.
  • The rules do not change: play a card, cover a matching space, two-eyed jacks are wild, one-eyed jacks remove a chip, the four corners are free.
  • A card-built board needs a big table, roughly 25 by 35 inches with standard cards.

Can you play Sequence with a regular deck of cards?

Yes, but one deck is not enough. Sequence has two parts: a hand of cards and a board with a picture of every card printed on it. The hand part is easy, because the cards in the box are ordinary playing cards. The game uses two standard decks shuffled together, 104 cards in all, with the jokers left out (see how many cards are in Sequence for the full count).

The board is what you have to make. On a real board, every card except the jacks appears twice, and the four corner spaces are free. That is 48 different cards × 2 = 96 spaces, plus 4 corners = 100 spaces on a 10x10 grid. Two extra decks with their 8 jacks removed give you exactly those 96 cards, so you can build the board out of real cards.

How do you set up a board with playing cards?

  1. Strip the jacks. Take two decks, remove all 8 jacks and the jokers. You should have 96 cards.
  2. Shuffle the board cards. Mix the 96 cards well so the two copies of each card end up in different parts of the grid.
  3. Lay out a 10x10 grid face up. Leave the four corner spots empty, or mark them with a coin or a sticky note. Those are the free corners.
  4. Check for clumps. If both copies of a card landed next to each other, swap one with a card from the other side of the table. Spread-out pairs make the game fairer.
  5. Prepare the draw pile. Shuffle the other two decks together (jacks in, jokers out) and deal hands based on the number of players.
  6. Hand out markers. Give each player or team one color of marker and start playing.

The printed Sequence board uses its own fixed arrangement, but the rules do not depend on a specific layout. Any grid where each card appears twice and the corners are free plays the same way. Can you play without laying out a board at all?

Not really. The whole game is about placing chips on a shared grid and blocking each other's lines, so you need something to place chips on. If four decks or a big table are not available, you can also draw or print a 10x10 grid on paper and write a card name in each square. A board drawn on paper is smaller and does not slide around the way loose cards do.

What are the rules when you use playing cards?

Exactly the same as the boxed game. Here is the short version:

  1. On your turn, play one card from your hand face up on your discard pile.
  2. Place one of your markers on a board card that matches it. Each card has two spaces on the board, and you can choose either one if it is open.
  3. Draw a new card from the draw pile. If you forget to draw before the next player draws, you lose that card for the rest of the game.
  4. A sequence is five of your markers in a row: across, down or diagonally. A free corner counts as one of your five.
  5. With 2 players or 2 teams you need two sequences to win; with 3 players or 3 teams you need one. Two sequences can share one space.

The full details, including table talk and what to do when the draw pile runs out, are on the Chipline rules page.

Which jacks are wild in a regular deck?

Because the board has no jacks, the jacks in your hand do special jobs. In a standard deck, the jack of diamonds and jack of clubs face forward and show two eyes. The jack of spades and jack of hearts are drawn in profile and show one eye.

  • Two-eyed jacks (diamonds, clubs) are wild. Place a marker on any open space on the board.
  • One-eyed jacks (spades, hearts) remove. Take one opponent marker off the board. You cannot remove a marker that is already part of a finished sequence, and you do not place a marker of your own that turn.

Some deck designs draw the jacks differently, so check yours before you start and agree which suits are wild. Our post on one-eyed vs two-eyed jacks covers the tricky cases.

What about dead cards and corners?

If both board copies of a card in your hand are already covered, the card is dead. Before you play, you may discard it, say so out loud, and draw a replacement, once per turn. The four corners belong to everyone, so a line through a corner needs only four markers. See the dead card rules and corner rules for examples.

What are the problems with a homemade card board?

It works, but a few things can go wrong. Knowing them in advance saves arguments:

  • Cards slide. A bumped table can shift a whole row. A tablecloth with some grip, or a strip of tape under each row, helps.
  • Markers roll. Coins and flat buttons stay put better than round beads.
  • Mixing decks. If board cards and hand cards have the same backs, they get mixed when you pack up. Keep them in separate boxes.
  • Reach. A board about three feet tall is hard to reach across. Sit around the long sides, or let a player call out a space for someone else to cover.
  • Setup time. Laying out 96 cards takes a few minutes. For a quick game, playing online skips setup completely.

Frequently asked questions

How many decks of cards do you need to play Sequence?

Two decks for the draw pile in any case. To build the board out of cards as well, you need two more, so four standard decks in total.

Do you use jokers when playing Sequence with playing cards?

No. Jokers are not used in standard Sequence. Remove them from every deck, both the board decks and the draw decks.

Why are there no jacks on the board?

Jacks are the special cards. Two-eyed jacks are wild and one-eyed jacks remove an opponent's chip, so they never need a space of their own. That is why the board is 96 cards plus 4 free corners.

Can I play Sequence with one deck of cards?

Not the standard way. One deck has only one copy of each card, but the board needs two copies of every non-jack card and the draw pile needs two full decks. With one deck you can make a smaller house-rule version, but it will not play like the real game.
