Sequence game app: how to play Sequence on your phone

Published 27 November 2026 · Chipline

Yes, you can play Sequence on your phone. You can install a Sequence-style app from the App Store or Google Play, or skip the download and play in your web browser. Browser games such as Chipline are free, need no account, work on iPhone and Android, and let friends join from a shared link.

Key takeaways

  • There are two ways to play Sequence on a phone: a downloaded app or a game that runs in the browser.
  • App store listings change often. Check the publisher, price, ads and in-app purchases before you install anything.
  • A browser game needs no download, takes no storage space and works the same on iPhone, Android, tablets and computers.
  • You can add a browser game to your home screen so it opens with one tap, just like an app.
  • Chipline is a free five-in-a-row card board game in the style of Sequence for 2 to 12 players, against the computer or with friends on their own phones.

Is there a Sequence game app?

Yes. If you search the App Store or Google Play for "Sequence", you will find several card board games built around the same idea: play a card, put a chip on the matching space of a 10x10 board, and race to make rows of five. Some are licensed versions of the boxed game, and others are independent games that play the same way under a different name.

What you find depends on your country and your device, and listings come and go. Prices, ads, in-app purchases and online features differ a lot from one app to the next. So instead of recommending a single app that may be gone next year, this guide shows you what to look for and how to play right now without installing anything.

How do you play Sequence on your phone without an app?

Here is how to start a game in the browser with Chipline. The same steps work on iPhone, Android, iPad and computers.

  1. Open the game. Go to playsequenceonline.github.io in Safari, Chrome or any other browser.
  2. Pick how you want to play. Play solo against computer players, pass one phone around the table, or create an online room for friends.
  3. Choose the players and teams. Games start with 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10 or 12 players, so everyone fits evenly into 2 or 3 sides.
  4. Invite friends (optional). Share the invite link on WhatsApp or any chat app, or read out the 5-letter room code. Each friend opens the link, types a name and joins.
  5. Play. Tap a card in your hand, then tap a highlighted space on the board to place your chip. The game deals, draws and checks for sequences for you.

How do you put a browser game on your home screen?

You can make a browser game feel like an app by adding a shortcut to your home screen. It then opens with one tap and fills the screen.

  • iPhone or iPad (Safari): open the game, tap the Share button, then tap Add to Home Screen and Add.
  • Android (Chrome): open the game, tap the three-dot menu, then tap Add to Home screen or Install app, depending on your version.

The menu wording varies a little between phone models and browser versions, but the option is in the share or main menu on every modern phone.

What should a good Sequence game app have?

Whether you pick an app or a browser game, these are the things worth checking before you invite the family:

  1. The standard board. A 10x10 grid where every card except the jacks appears twice, plus four free corners that count for everyone. See the board layout guide for how the 100 spaces work.
  2. Correct jack rules. Two-eyed jacks are wild and let you place a chip on any open space. One-eyed jacks remove an opponent's chip, but never one that is already part of a finished sequence.
  3. The right win condition. With 2 players or 2 teams you need two sequences. With 3 players or 3 teams you need one. A sequence is five chips in a row across, down or diagonally.
  4. Dead card handling. If both spaces for a card are taken, you should be able to swap it for a new one, once per turn.
  5. Enough player counts. Teams of 2 or 3 sides, from 2 up to 12 players, if you play with big groups.
  6. Easy online play. A way for friends to join quickly, ideally across iPhone and Android.
  7. Fair ads and privacy. Look at what data the app collects and whether ads interrupt every turn.

If an app gets the jacks or the win condition wrong, you will learn bad habits for the real board. The full Sequence rules are a good reference to check against.

Can you play Sequence with friends on different phones?

Yes, and this is where playing on phones is most useful. In an online room, each player sees their own hand on their own screen, so nobody can peek at anyone else's cards. You can be in the same living room or in different cities.

In Chipline, the host starts a room, picks the teams and a turn timer, and can fill empty seats with computer players. Friends on iPhone, Android or a laptop all join the same game from the link. If you are a large group, our guide to Sequence for 6 to 12 players explains how to split into teams.

What if only one phone is available?

Use pass-and-play. Everyone shares one device and hands it to the next player after each turn. It works well for two players or a small family at the kitchen table. For bigger groups, separate phones are faster and keep hands private.

Is playing Sequence on a phone the same as the board game?

The rules are the same, and the game feels very close. A few things are different:

  • No setup or cleanup. The game shuffles, deals and draws for you, and you never lose a chip under the sofa.
  • No missed draws. On the real board you lose a card if you forget to draw before the next player does. In most apps the draw is automatic.
  • Rules are enforced. You cannot place a chip on the wrong space or remove a chip that is locked in a sequence.
  • Less table talk. Teammates cannot whisper hints when they are on different phones, which is how the official rules want it anyway.
  • The board is smaller. On a phone you will zoom in or turn the screen to see all 100 spaces clearly.

The strategy carries over completely. Blocking, building lines through the corners and saving your jacks matter just as much on a screen. Our strategy tips and the guide to one-eyed vs two-eyed jacks will help you win more online games.

Tips for playing Sequence on a small screen

  • Look at the whole board before tapping a card. It is easy to focus on your own line and miss an opponent who has four in a row.
  • Use the highlighted spaces. When you select a card, the game shows where it can go, including the second copy of that card that you may have missed.
  • Keep a two-eyed jack for the end of the game. A wild card is often the only way to finish your last sequence.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an official Sequence app?

Licensed Sequence apps have been published for phones, but app store listings change over time and differ by country. Search the App Store or Google Play and check the publisher name and reviews before installing. Many independent apps and browser games play the same way under other names.

Can I play a Sequence-style game on my phone for free?

Yes. Some apps are free with ads or in-app purchases. Chipline is free in your phone's browser with no download, no account and no sign-up, for 2 to 12 players.

Can iPhone and Android users play Sequence together?

Only if the app supports cross-platform play. Browser games avoid the problem completely, because everyone opens the same web page. In Chipline, iPhone, Android and computer players can all join one room from the same link.

Can you play Sequence on your phone against the computer?

Yes. Most Sequence-style apps and Chipline include computer players, so you can practice alone or fill empty seats when your group has an odd number of people.

Can you play Sequence on a phone without internet?

Only with an app that has an offline mode. A browser game needs a connection to load, and online games with friends need everyone to be connected. Without any signal, the physical board or a homemade version with regular playing cards is the way to go.

Do the rules change when you play Sequence in an app?

No. A good app uses the same rules as the board game: a 10x10 board with four free corners, two-eyed jacks wild, one-eyed jacks remove a chip, five in a row makes a sequence, and the number of sequences you need depends on the number of players or teams. See the Chipline FAQ for more.
