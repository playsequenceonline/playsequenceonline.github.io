How many cards are in Sequence? Deck size and cards per hand

Published 21 November 2026 · Chipline

Sequence uses 104 cards: two standard 52-card decks shuffled together, with the jokers removed. How many cards you hold depends on the number of players: 7 each with 2 players, 6 with 3 or 4, 5 with 6, 4 with 8 or 9, and 3 with 10 or 12. You keep the same hand size all game by drawing after every turn.

Key takeaways

  • The deck is two standard decks (104 cards). Jokers are not used.
  • Every card except the jacks appears twice on the board, so each card in your hand has two possible spaces.
  • There are 8 jacks in the deck: 4 two-eyed (wild) and 4 one-eyed (remove a chip).
  • Hand size goes down as player count goes up, from 7 cards with 2 players to 3 cards with 10 or 12.
  • You draw one card at the end of each turn, so your hand stays the same size until the draw pile runs out.

How many cards are in a Sequence deck?

A Sequence deck has 104 cards: two full 52-card decks shuffled together. The two jokers from each deck are left in the box.

Why two decks? The board has 100 spaces. The four corners are free spaces that belong to everyone, which leaves 96 spaces for cards. A single deck without jacks has 48 cards (12 ranks in 4 suits), and each of those 48 cards is printed on the board twice. That is 96 spaces, and it means you need two copies of every card in the deck so both spaces can be claimed.

The jacks are the only cards with no space on the board. Instead they act as special cards, which is why the deck still includes all 8 of them. If you want to see exactly where each card sits, our guide to the Sequence game board layout walks through the 10x10 grid.

How many cards do you get in Sequence?

The last two columns are simple arithmetic (players × cards each, then 104 minus that), but they are handy to know: even in a 12-player game, more than two-thirds of the deck is still waiting to be drawn when play starts.

The number of sequences needed to win also changes with the teams: two players or two teams need two sequences, while three players or three teams need only one. The full table is on our rules page.

Why do bigger games get fewer cards?

Two reasons. First, in a team game your partners hold cards too, so your team as a whole still has plenty of options. Four players with 6 cards each means each team controls 12 cards between them. Second, fewer cards per person keeps the game moving. With 12 players, a hand of 7 would mean long pauses while everyone scans the board, and far fewer surprises.

The trade-off is that in big games each player has less control. With only 3 cards, you may have nothing useful to play on a given turn, which is why team play and reading your partners' moves matters so much. Our guide to playing Sequence with 6 to 12 players covers seating and team tips.

How do you deal and draw cards in Sequence?

  1. Shuffle both decks together. Remove the jokers first, then mix the 104 cards well.
  2. Deal the right number face down. Use the table above. Keep your hand hidden, including from your teammates.
  3. Place the rest face down as the draw pile. Leave room next to it for a discard pile.
  4. On your turn, play one card. Put it face up on the discard pile and place a chip on a matching space (or use a jack).
  5. Draw one card to end your turn. This brings your hand back to its starting size.

The draw step is easy to forget in a lively game. In the published rules, if you forget to draw and the next player has already taken their turn, you lose the right to draw and play the rest of the game with one card fewer. Many families relax this at home, but it is worth agreeing on before you start.

What happens if the draw pile runs out?

With 68 to 90 cards in the draw pile, most games end long before the deck runs dry. If it does happen, the common approach is to shuffle the discard pile and turn it over as a new draw pile, then carry on. Since rule sheets vary between editions, agree on this as a table rule if your games tend to run long or end in stalemates.

What is a dead card, and does it change your hand size?

A dead card is a card whose two board spaces are both already covered by chips, so it can never be played. On your turn you can put a dead card on the discard pile, say so out loud, and draw a replacement before making your normal play. You can do this once per turn, and your hand size stays the same. The details and edge cases are in our post on Sequence dead card rules.

Can you play Sequence with one deck of cards?

Not with the standard board. Each card has two spaces, so with a single 52-card deck half the board could never be claimed by playing a card, and the game would stall. If you have lost cards from a set, you can rebuild it from any two ordinary decks, ideally with matching backs so nobody can tell cards apart from behind. Remember to take out the jokers. How to use your hand size to play better

Knowing the card counts helps your strategy, not just your setup:

  • Count jacks. There are only 4 of each type. Once you have seen three two-eyed jacks played, your opponents can hold at most one more.
  • Watch the second copy. Every card has exactly two copies and two spaces. If one copy has been played and you hold the other, you control the last open space for that card.
  • Plan for the hand you have. With 7 cards (2 players) you can build two threats at once. With 3 cards (10 or 12 players) you will often play the best card available, so lean on your team.
  • Swap dead cards quickly. A dead card shrinks your real options, and the smaller your hand, the more that hurts.

Frequently asked questions

How many cards are in a Sequence deck?

104 cards: two standard 52-card decks shuffled together, with the jokers removed.

How many cards do you get with 2 players in Sequence?

Each player gets 7 cards when two people play head to head, and each player needs two sequences to win.

How many cards do you get with 4 players in Sequence?

Each player gets 6 cards. Four players play as 2 teams of 2, and each team needs two sequences to win.

How many jacks are in Sequence?

There are 8 jacks: 4 two-eyed jacks (diamonds and clubs) that are wild, and 4 one-eyed jacks (spades and hearts) that remove an opponent's chip.

Are jokers used in Sequence?

No. Standard Sequence rules do not use jokers, so take them out before you shuffle.

Do you draw a card after every turn in Sequence?

Yes. After playing a card you draw one from the draw pile, so your hand stays the same size. If you forget and the next player has played, the published rules say you lose that draw.
