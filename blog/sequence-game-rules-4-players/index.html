Sequence game rules for 4 players: how to play in two teams

Published November 18, 2026 · Chipline

With 4 players, Sequence is played as 2 teams of 2. Partners sit across from each other, everyone gets 6 cards, and a team needs two sequences of five chips in a row to win. Teammates share one chip color but may not talk about their cards. Everything else follows the normal rules.

Key takeaways

  • Four players always play as two teams of two, one chip color per team.
  • Deal 6 cards to each player and draw one card at the end of every turn.
  • Sit partners across from each other so the teams take turns: A, B, A, B.
  • A team wins with two sequences of five. The two lines may share one chip.
  • Two-eyed jacks are wild; one-eyed jacks remove an opponent's chip; the four corners are free for everyone.
  • No table talk: partners "talk" only through the chips they place.

How do you play Sequence with 4 players?

The four-player game uses the same board, deck and turn order as every other version of Sequence. What changes is that you are no longer on your own. You and a partner form a team, share a chip color and win or lose together.

That makes four players one of the most popular ways to play. It feels like a classic card-table partnership game: you cannot see your partner's hand, so you have to read their moves on the board and build on them.

How do you set up a 4-player game?

  1. Pick teams. Split into two teams of two. Each team chooses one chip color.
  2. Sit in alternating order. Partners sit across the table, so the order around the table is Team A, Team B, Team A, Team B.
  3. Shuffle the deck. Sequence uses two standard 52-card decks, 104 cards in total. Leave any jokers out.
  4. Deal 6 cards to each player. Put the rest face down as the draw pile.
  5. Start the game. The player to the dealer's left goes first, and play continues to the left.

What happens on each turn?

A turn has three parts, and they are the same for every player count:

  1. Play a card from your hand onto your own discard pile.
  2. Place a chip of your team's color on a matching space. Every non-jack card appears twice on the board, so you can choose either copy if both are open.
  3. Draw a card so you are back to 6. If you forget and the next player has already drawn, you lose that draw and play on with fewer cards.

If both spaces for a card are already covered, it is a dead card. At the start of your turn, discard it, draw a replacement, then take your turn as normal.

How do the jacks work with teams?

Jacks have no space on the board. A two-eyed jack (diamonds and clubs) is wild: place your team's chip on any open space. A one-eyed jack (spades and hearts) removes one chip of the other team's color, as long as that chip is not already part of a finished sequence. You cannot use a one-eyed jack to remove your partner's chip, because it is your color too. For more detail, see one-eyed vs two-eyed jacks.

How many sequences do you need to win with 4 players?

Two. With two teams, the first team to complete two sequences of five chips in a row wins. Rows, columns and diagonals all count.

Two details matter. First, your two sequences may share one chip, so an L, T or X shape of nine chips can count as two lines. Second, the four corner spaces count as a chip of every color, so a line that runs into a corner needs only four of your own chips.

Can 4 people play Sequence without teams?

Not under the standard rules. The game comes with chips in three colors, so it supports up to three separate sides. With four people, the rules call for two teams of two. If someone really wants a solo game, the usual fix is a 3-player game with one person sitting out, which uses the 3-player rules (6 cards each, one sequence wins).

How is 4-player Sequence different from 2-player?

Both games are two sides racing to two sequences, but they play very differently.

In a two-player game you control every chip of your color. With four, half of your team's chips come from someone else's hand, and the board can change a lot before your next turn. The 2-player rules guide covers the head-to-head game in detail.

What is the best strategy for 4-player Sequence?

The general strategy tips still apply. These are the ones that matter most when you play with a partner.

Build where your partner is building

Your partner cannot tell you their plan, but their chips can. If they place a chip next to one of yours, they are probably holding cards for that line. Add to it instead of starting a new one somewhere else.

Leave "open" spaces your partner can fill

A line with gaps on both ends gives your partner more cards that help. A line pressed against an opponent's chip gives them fewer options.

Block before the second opponent plays

Between your turns, both opponents play. A line of three can become four, and then five, before you see it again. Block threats early, especially four in a row with an open end.

Save your jacks for the big moments

A two-eyed jack is best used to finish a line or block one that you cannot stop any other way. A one-eyed jack is best used on a chip that sits in the middle of an opponent's line of four.

Do not wreck your partner's plan

If your partner just played into an open area, avoid filling the space next to it with a random chip of your own unless it helps the same line. Think "team line" before "my line."

Frequently asked questions

How many cards do you deal with 4 players in Sequence?

Deal 6 cards to each player, 24 cards in total. Everyone keeps 6 cards in hand for the whole game by drawing one card at the end of every turn.

Can 4 people play Sequence without teams?

Not under the standard rules. The game comes with chips in only three colors, so four solo players would not each have a color. Four players split into 2 teams of 2, and each team shares one color.

How many sequences does a team need to win with 4 players?

Two. With 2 teams, the first team to complete two sequences of five wins. The two lines may share one chip, and the free corners count for every team.

How should partners sit in a 4-player Sequence game?

Partners sit across from each other so the teams alternate around the table: Team A, Team B, Team A, Team B. Play then passes to the left, and no one plays twice in a row for the same team.

Can partners talk about their cards in Sequence?

No. Partners may not show their cards, say what they hold or hint at which space to play. You can only communicate through the chips you place on the board.
