Sequence game board layout: how the 100 spaces work

Published November 19, 2026 · Chipline

The Sequence game board is a 10x10 grid of 100 spaces. The four corners are free spaces that count for everyone, and the other 96 spaces show every card from a standard deck except the jacks, each one twice. Jacks are not on the board because they are the special wild and remove cards.

Key takeaways

  • The board is 10 rows by 10 columns: 100 spaces in total.
  • 96 spaces show cards: the 48 non-jack cards of a deck, each printed twice.
  • The 4 corners are free and count as a chip of every color.
  • No jacks appear on the board. Two-eyed jacks are wild; one-eyed jacks remove a chip.
  • The two copies of a card sit in different parts of the board, so every card gives you a choice.

What does the Sequence board look like?

Picture a square grid, ten spaces across and ten spaces down. Every space except the four corners shows a playing card: an ace, a number card, a queen or a king, in one of the four suits. When you play a card from your hand, you put one of your chips on a free space that shows the same card.

The board is played with two standard 52-card decks shuffled together, so 104 cards. Each card in the decks matches a picture on the board, except the 8 jacks, which have special powers instead of a space.

How many spaces are on a Sequence board?

There are 100 spaces. Here is how they add up:

Free corner spaces4One in each corner; they count for every player
Jack spaces0Jacks are action cards and have no space
Total100A 10x10 grid

Why does every card appear twice on the board?

Because the game uses two decks, there are two copies of every card, and each copy needs somewhere to go. Printing each card twice also makes the game more interesting: when you hold the 4 of clubs, you can choose between two spaces. One might extend your own line while the other blocks an opponent.

It also creates the idea of a dead card. If both spaces for a card are already covered, that card can never be played. On your turn you discard it, draw a new card, and then play as normal. The full rules explain dead cards step by step.

Why are there no jacks on the Sequence board?

Jacks are the special cards. The two-eyed jacks (diamonds and clubs) are wild, so you can place a chip on any free space. The one-eyed jacks (spades and hearts) let you remove one opponent chip that is not already part of a finished sequence. Since a jack never needs a matching space, the 8 jacks are left off the board entirely. Our guide to one-eyed vs two-eyed jacks goes into when to play each one.

How do the free corners work?

The four corner spaces have no card. They act as if a chip of every color is already sitting there. Any player or team can count a corner as one of the five spaces in a line, so you only need four of your own chips to finish a line that runs into a corner. Nobody ever places a chip on a corner, and a one-eyed jack cannot remove it.

That makes the edges near the corners valuable. A line along the top row, the side column or a long diagonal that touches a corner is one chip shorter than a line in the middle of the board.

How is a sequence made on the board?

A sequence is five of your chips in a straight line: across a row, down a column or on a diagonal. The corners can be one of the five. How many sequences you need depends on how many sides are playing:

  1. 2 players or 2 teams: make two sequences to win. The two lines may share one chip.
  2. 3 players or 3 teams: make one sequence to win.
  3. Once a sequence is finished, its chips are safe and cannot be removed by a one-eyed jack.

For player-count specifics, see rules for 2 players and rules for 4 players.

Is the Sequence board layout the same in every game?

On a printed board, yes. The cardboard board has one fixed arrangement, so people who play often start to remember where cards sit. That knowledge helps: if you know both 9 of spades spaces are on the left side, you know a 9 of spades can never block a line on the right.

In Chipline, the free online version you can play in your browser, every game deals a fresh, balanced board. Each card still appears exactly twice and the corners are still free, but the two copies of a card are always kept apart: never in the same row or the same column, and never within two spaces of each other. So no one gets an edge from memorizing the board, and every game feels new.

How do you make your own Sequence-style board?

If you want a homemade board for family game night, the layout rules above are all you need:

  1. Draw a 10x10 grid on a large sheet of paper or poster board. Squares of about 2 inches (5 cm) are easy to read across a table.
  2. Mark the four corners as free spaces.
  3. Write in the 48 non-jack cards twice, for 96 spaces. Leave the jacks off.
  4. Keep the two copies apart. Avoid putting matching cards in the same row, column or right next to each other, so each card gives a real choice.
  5. Check your count: every non-jack card should appear exactly twice, and you should have no empty squares except the corners.

Use two ordinary decks for the draw pile and coins, buttons or counters in two or three colors as chips.

Tips for reading the board during a game

  • Look for both spaces before you play. One copy of your card is often far more useful than the other.
  • Count what is left. If one copy of a card is covered, only one more chance remains for anyone to take that spot.
  • Use the corners. Lines that end in a corner need only four chips.
  • Watch the center. Middle spaces sit on more possible lines than edge spaces, so they are useful for both building and blocking.

Frequently asked questions

How many squares are on a Sequence board?

100 squares in a 10x10 grid: 96 card spaces plus 4 free corner spaces.

Are there jacks on the Sequence board?

No. Jacks are not printed on the board. Two-eyed jacks are wild and one-eyed jacks remove an opponent's chip, so they never need a space.

How many times does each card appear on the Sequence board?

Twice. Every card except the jacks is shown on two different spaces, which matches the two decks used in the game.

What are the corners on a Sequence board for?

The four corners are free spaces that count as a chip for every player or team. You can use a corner as one of the five spaces in your line.
