How to play Sequence against the computer

Open Chipline and tap Play the computer. You are red, the computer is blue, and the first side to make two lines of five chips wins.

  1. Open Chipline. Go to playsequenceonline.github.io on your phone, tablet or computer.
  2. Tap Play the computer. The cards are dealt straight away. You get 7 cards, the same as the computer.
  3. Play a card. Tap a card in your hand and the board lights up the spaces you can use. Tap one to drop your chip, and you draw a new card automatically.
  4. Make two lines of five. Lines can run across, down or diagonally, and the four star corners count for you. Watch the computer's lines and block them before they reach five.

New to the game? Read the rules of Sequence, explained simply, including what the one-eyed and two-eyed jacks do.

How the computer plays

The computer plays to win, not to pass time. It weighs every space on the board each turn, adding up how much a move helps its own lines and how much it blocks yours.

  • It blocks you. Get three or four chips in a row and expect the computer to drop a chip in the gap.
  • It saves its jacks. It keeps two-eyed jacks (wild) for winning or saving moves, and one-eyed jacks (remove a chip) for real threats, instead of spending them early.
  • It likes the middle. Centre spaces sit in more possible lines, so the computer drifts toward them.
  • It never cheats. It only sees its own hand and the board, and it draws from the same shuffled deck as you. It swaps dead cards by the same rule you do.

5 tips to beat the computer

  1. Build two threats at once. The computer can block one line per turn. A move that pushes two of your lines to four forces it to leave one open.
  2. Use the corners. Each star corner counts as your chip, so a line through a corner needs only four of yours.
  3. Make it spend its jacks. It holds its jacks for big moments. Threaten early on one side of the board, then win on the other.
  4. Keep your own jacks. A two-eyed jack finishes a line anywhere. Save it for your fifth chip rather than your first.
  5. Swap dead cards. If both spaces for a card are taken, swap it at the start of your turn so you never waste a turn.

Questions about playing against the computer

Can I play Sequence against the computer online?

Yes. Open Chipline and tap Play the computer. A game against one computer player starts straight away, free, in your browser, with no download or sign-up.

Is the computer player hard to beat?

It plays a solid game: it builds its own lines, blocks yours when you have three or four in a row, and saves its jacks for important moves. Beginners can win by watching for its blocks and building two lines at once.

Can I play with a friend against the computer?

Yes. Choose Pass one phone around, tap Change teams or add computer players, and pick 4 players, then set seats 1 and 3 to Person and seats 2 and 4 to Computer. Seats alternate between teams, so you and your friend play as a team against two computer players.

Can I add computer players to an online game?

Yes. In an online room the host can tap Add computer player to fill any empty seat before the game starts. If a friend leaves mid-game, a computer takes over their seat.

Do I need the internet to play against the computer?

You need it to open the page. After that, a game against the computer runs in your browser, and your game is saved on your device so you can continue it later.

What happens if I close the page in the middle of a game?

Your game against the computer is saved on your device. Open Chipline again and tap Continue your game to pick up where you left off.

Does the computer cheat or see my cards?

No. The computer only knows its own hand and the board, the same as you. It draws from the same shuffled deck.

How many computer players can I add?

Any seat can be a computer player, as long as at least one seat is a person. For example, you can play 1 against 2 computers in a three-sided game, or with 2 computer teammates against 3 computer opponents.
